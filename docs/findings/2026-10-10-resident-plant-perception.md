# Residents can notice nearby instanced plants

The asset-upgrade request action already accepts vegetation targets, but the ordinary nearby-prop summary intentionally excludes instanced components. Consequently, residents could suggest a world-wide vegetation change but had little factual context for a specific plant patch.

`UAgentBrainComponent::BuildSituationSummary` now checks visible HISM components owned by `AIslandWeather` within a 6.5 m radius. It uses each component's sphere-overlap query, examines at most 48 nearby instance transforms and traces visibility to at most three candidates per component, then reports no more than the three nearest distinct mesh names. The description uses the resident-relative direction and explicitly says this is descriptive `request_upgrade` context only—not a stable object ID or `move_to`/`interact` target. Hidden components and plants behind visibility-blocking geometry are omitted. It neither calls a model nor writes memory, world state, or the request queue.

The Island's numbered foliage-pack asset IDs are now described by their visible category rather than their implementation names: `flower_XX_XX` as wildflowers, `ground_XX_XX` as low groundcover, spruce assets as spruce trees, and the two named grass meshes as grasses. Unknown assets retain a sanitized name with a generic vegetation label; the summary does not claim a species identity it cannot verify. Regression coverage uses transient duplicated meshes named like the Island assets and confirms they produce perceptual wording instead of numeric IDs. It also checks the installed numbered flower and ground-cover meshes, an interactive spruce, Festuca, and Phalaris when those optional assets are present.

The source-linked UE 5.8.3 scratch project built successfully with its own `Binaries` while the separate Claude editor remained open. `CaptiveSky2.Agent.NearbyVegetationPerception` passed under `NullRHI`, a 60-second real-time cap, disabled agent thinking, and a zero model-request cap. Its transient fixture verifies a nearby visible HISM mesh is described, its suggestion cannot become a movement or interaction target, a solid occluder suppresses it, and moving beyond the short radius removes it. After the vocabulary refinement, the isolated target rebuilt successfully (two changed C++ units) and the same automation passed again, including the numbered-flower and numbered-groundcover labels. A further isolated rebuild recompiled the actual-mesh checks, and the same automation passed with the project's installed Fab foliage, spruce, Festuca, and Phalaris packages available. This test proves the bounded perception and vocabulary rules on a small fixture, not the cost or visibility of a full Island scatter with roughly 1.8 million instances, or how natural the descriptions sound beside an imported native plant asset. The next check is a matched prompt-construction timing sample on the saved Island, then a bounded in-world check of labels and visibility with a real plant. Until that evidence exists, the descriptive scan remains limited to the nearest 6.5 m.
