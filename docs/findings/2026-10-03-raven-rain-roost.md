# Raven roost choice in heavy rain (2026-10-03)

The raven already receives two separate kinds of local roost evidence: measured wind shelter and five short visibility probes above the perch marker. The overhead probes are only a small rain-cover clue; they do not prove that a site is dry, strong, or safe. Before this change, opportunistic `BeginPerch()` considered wind and travel distance, but ignored those already-measured overhead clues.

The selector now samples current rain intensity and each physically supported roost's overhead probe count. Only during a strong shower (rain intensity at least 0.55) can a site with at least two more overhead hits than the wind-aware choice change the preference. Among sites with nearly the best cover count, the existing wind-and-distance selector still decides. Missing or mismatched cover samples, no meaningful cover difference, and lighter rain all preserve the existing wind-aware/nearest behavior. This acts only after the raven has already chosen an action that may perch; it does not force him to seek shelter or add model requests.

The situation summary and system prompt describe the evidence as a clue rather than a guarantee. The saved Island currently has two supported roosts, and both report 0 of 5 overhead hits, so this selection rule does not change their present preference. A genuinely sheltered raven perch would be needed to exercise a different choice on this map.

Deterministic assertions cover heavy-rain preference, light-rain fallback, insufficient cover difference, wind choice between similarly covered sites, and malformed evidence.
